## Design Context

### Users
Recruiters, potential employers, collaborators, and fellow developers visiting Floyd Benedikter's personal portfolio. They're evaluating technical skill, creativity, and personality — often scanning quickly before deciding to dig deeper. Context: desktop during work hours, mobile during commutes.

### Brand Personality
**Bold, experimental, playful.** This isn't a safe corporate portfolio — it's a statement piece that shows Floyd takes risks and has taste. The site should feel like opening a creative person's sketchbook, not reading a resume PDF.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Visual tone**: Creative portfolio — art-directed, visually surprising, Awwwards-caliber. Each section should feel intentionally composed, not templated.
- **References**: Awwwards winners, creative agency portfolios, art-directed editorial sites. The kind of site where you scroll slowly because you don't want to miss anything.
- **Anti-references**: Generic developer portfolios with card grids, timeline components, and dark-mode-with-cyan. Nothing that looks like a template or a "developer portfolio starter kit."
- **Theme**: Dark-first (already established with 6 dark themes), but dark done with intention — not the lazy "dark + neon accent" AI default.

### Design Principles
1. **Surprise over safety** — Every section should have at least one moment that makes you pause. Unexpected layouts, bold typography choices, or interactive elements that reward curiosity.
2. **Personality is the product** — This site sells Floyd as a person, not just a skill set. Let quirks, humor, and taste come through in every design decision.
3. **Craft over convention** — Reject the first "good enough" solution. If a layout feels like something you've seen before, redesign it.
4. **Rhythm over uniformity** — Vary scale, spacing, and density across sections. A portfolio should feel like a curated exhibition, not a spreadsheet.
5. **Accessible boldness** — WCAG AA compliance. Bold design doesn't mean excluding anyone — high contrast, keyboard navigation, and semantic HTML are non-negotiable.
